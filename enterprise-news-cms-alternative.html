Enterprise news CMS or a self-hosted CMS?

Arc XP, Brightspot, RebelMouse, and Quintype power large digital newsrooms. If your publication is small or regional, you may not need, or be able to afford, that kind of platform. Here is how the options compare.

The enterprise news platforms

Arc XP

The digital experience platform that grew out of The Washington Post. A closed, cloud-hosted suite for large, fast-moving newsrooms, licensed in modules.

Brightspot

An enterprise CMS that markets itself to media organizations, including large wire and broadcast newsrooms. Setup usually involves planning, training, and workflow design.

RebelMouse

A hosted publishing platform and site-build service for digital publishers, with hosting and CDN bundled into the price.

Quintype Bold

An India-based, headless news CMS used by publishers in Asia and elsewhere. Pricing is based on bandwidth, with a setup fee.

All four are capable products built for organizations with budgets and technical teams to match. VonCMS is different: free, open source software that you install on ordinary PHP and MySQL hosting.

What you gain and give up

Managed enterprise platform vs self-hosted CMS
What mattersEnterprise platformVonCMS, self-hosted
Hosting and uptimeRun by the vendor, usually with a service agreement.Your hosting provider and you. Uptime depends on the host you choose.
SupportAccount management and support included or available.Documentation and the open source project. No support contract.
ScaleProven at very high traffic with CDN and caching.Suited to small and regional publications on standard hosting. Test with your expected traffic.
Newsroom workflowDeep, configurable workflows, often across many desks and brands.Writer submissions, staff review, scheduling, and an editorial calendar for a single publication.
CustomizationThrough the vendor's APIs, services, or partners. Changes may need paid work.Edit the source directly, or hire any PHP developer.
Data and exitHosted on the vendor's platform. Plan how you would export content.Your database and files sit on your own server.

The Google News Initiative guide notes that relying on a vendor for frequent customizations can lead to lock-in. A self-hosted CMS removes that dependency, but replaces it with your own responsibility for updates, backups, and security.

Choose an enterprise platform if…

  • You run several brands or desks and need complex, multi-step workflows.
  • You expect very high traffic and want the vendor to own uptime and scaling.
  • You have a budget for licensing, implementation, and ongoing services.
  • You need integrations, subscriptions, or apps that the platform already supports.

Choose a self-hosted CMS like VonCMS if…

  • You are an independent, local, or regional publication with a small team.
  • License fees would take budget away from reporting.
  • You want your content, database, and source code under your own control.
  • You or a developer you trust can manage hosting, backups, and updates.

Many newsrooms in this position also consider WordPress or a managed WordPress platform. See WordPress vs VonCMS and Newspack vs a self-hosted CMS.

Questions to ask before you choose

  1. What will the platform cost in total over three years, including implementation and services?
  2. Who will handle hosting, updates, security, and backups?
  3. How many people need access, and what roles do they need?
  4. How would you export your articles, images, and URLs if you leave?
  5. Which features do you use every day, and which are nice to have?
